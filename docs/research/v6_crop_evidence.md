# V6 crop evidence: garlic chives and sawtooth coriander

Research cutoff: 9 September 2026. This was a targeted source review, not a systematic review or a commercial growing recipe.

## Selection and identity

The catalogue adds two species and preserves all ten earlier profiles and the original four synthetic-farm crops. `garlic_chives` is *Allium tuberosum* Rottler ex Spreng. (Amaryllidaceae). `sawtooth_coriander` is *Eryngium foetidum* L. (Apiaceae). Neither species, genus nor family occurred in the previous ten-profile portfolio. They complement the existing leafy vegetables as repeated-cut culinary herbs with visibly different growth forms.

Singapore's National Parks Board supplies the identity and local cultivation background. Its [garlic-chives record](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/4/9/4947) describes a perennial, grass-like loose clump of flat, pointed, parallel-veined leaves, with edible leaves, stems and flowers; it reports a tropical preference, full sun or semi-shade, moderate water and well-drained fertile soil. Its [sawtooth-coriander record](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/4/8/4850) describes a low rosette of long, finely toothed leaves and explicitly says the species is better adapted to Singapore's climate than true coriander, which tends to flower early. It also warns that leaves become tougher after flowering. These extension statements establish useful local background, not commercial farm coefficients.

The common-name boundaries matter. “Chives” can refer to other *Allium* species. “Coriander” usually refers to *Coriandrum sativum*, while “Vietnamese coriander” is also used for *Persicaria odorata*. FarmTact therefore keeps those inputs unresolved until a scientific name or product description identifies the crop.

## Primary research and usable findings

For garlic chives, Kale et al., [doi:10.3389/fpls.2024.1480510](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2024.1480510), followed three accessions in an Indian field study across two years and repeated cuts. Accession and seasonal window affected growth and quality, while larger clumps and more tillers accompanied both foliage production and harvest waste. The result supports separate fields for accession, season, crop age, cut number, saleable foliage and waste. It does not transfer the paper's spacing, cutting schedule or yield to Singapore.

Liu et al., [doi:10.3389/fnut.2022.1000271](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2022.1000271), tested one Chinese-chive cultivar under controlled hydroponic salinity treatments in Beijing. Growth, mineral composition, metabolites and flavour-related compounds responded to treatment. This supports keeping biomass and quality endpoints separate. It does not authorize salt addition or a Singapore nutrient recipe.

For sawtooth coriander, Mozumder et al., [doi:10.29121/granthaalayah.v8.i12.2020.2714](https://doi.org/10.29121/granthaalayah.v8.i12.2020.2714), compared shade environments and sowing methods at two Bangladesh research stations. The study supports capturing shade structure, sowing method, location, season, flowering and fresh-leaf endpoint together. Its field yields do not transfer directly to Singapore or hydroponics.

The Brazilian field study [doi:10.1590/s0102-0536-2023-e2554](https://doi.org/10.1590/s0102-0536-2023-e2554) compared full sun with graded shade and reported treatment-specific leaf-area, biomass and production responses. It reinforces that shade must remain an observed treatment linked to a defined endpoint. One location cannot determine a universal shade target, cultivar response, infrastructure choice or farm economics.

## Trade mapping, image reuse and limits

The Singapore Food Agency's [fresh fruit and vegetable product-code list](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/docs/default-source/tools-and-resources/product-codes-for-tradenet/product-code-list-for-fresh-fruits-and-vegetables.pdf) includes Sawtooth Coriander (Culantro), product code `HVL0PSC`, under broad HS code `07099990`. The product description can identify a declared commodity, but the HS code alone cannot produce a crop-specific SingStat series. No specific SFA garlic-chives product entry was verified, so broad alliaceous code `07039090` remains unresolved.

NParks photographs and comparison graphics were used as botanical visual references only. Their reuse licence was not established, so no image was copied or redistributed. The research articles are represented by short attributed findings and metadata; no full article, table, figure or dataset is embedded. Every numerical production parameter remains blocked until its endpoint, units, cultivar, system, local applicability and independent validation are established.
